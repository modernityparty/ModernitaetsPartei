# Modernity Party
## Political Programme
### Version 0.1 — Open Programme Draft

**Politics for the modern world.**

## 1. Purpose

Modernity Party seeks to develop political approaches that enable people and societies to respond effectively to the challenges and opportunities of the modern world.

Our programme is based on human dignity, individual freedom, democratic accountability, reason, scientific knowledge and continuous institutional improvement.

This document establishes our initial policy framework. It is open to public discussion, research, amendment and democratic adoption.

## 2. Government and Public Administration

We seek public institutions that are accessible, efficient, transparent and accountable.

Our objectives:

- Modernise administrative processes.
- Reduce unnecessary bureaucracy.
- Establish digital-first public services without excluding people who need analogue access.
- Improve interoperability between public institutions.
- Make public expenditure and institutional responsibilities understandable.
- Evaluate public services against measurable outcomes.
- Strengthen independent oversight and accountability.

## 3. Democracy and Political Participation

Democracy must be continuous, informed and accessible.

We propose:

- Better access to legislative information.
- Transparent documentation of political decisions.
- Accessible public consultations.
- Digital participation mechanisms with appropriate security and privacy safeguards.
- Strong parliamentary oversight.
- Transparent political financing.
- Protection of independent journalism and freedom of expression.

Digital participation must complement, not replace, constitutional democratic institutions.

## 4. Education and Knowledge

Education is a lifelong process and a fundamental condition of individual and collective development.

We support:

- High-quality, accessible education.
- Strong foundations in mathematics, science, languages and critical thinking.
- Digital and media literacy.
- Scientific literacy and research competence.
- Vocational education and professional development.
- Lifelong learning opportunities.
- Greater cooperation between educational institutions, research and industry.
- Academic freedom and institutional independence.

## 5. Economy and Innovation

Economic policy should enable prosperity, opportunity, innovation and responsible resource allocation.

We propose:

- Improving conditions for entrepreneurship.
- Reducing unnecessary regulatory burdens.
- Supporting research and development.
- Encouraging responsible technological innovation.
- Promoting competition and preventing abusive market concentration.
- Strengthening economic resilience.
- Improving access to professional education and employment.
- Evaluating economic policy through productivity, prosperity and quality of life.

## 6. Science and Technology

Scientific progress and technological development should serve humanity.

We support:

- Independent scientific research.
- Open scientific knowledge wherever legally and ethically possible.
- Responsible artificial intelligence development.
- Cybersecurity and resilient digital infrastructure.
- Digital sovereignty and interoperability.
- Transparent public-sector technology procurement.
- Ethical assessment of emerging technologies.
- Public access to publicly funded research results.

## 7. Justice and the Rule of Law

Justice must be independent, accessible, predictable and effective.

We seek:

- Equal treatment before the law.
- Effective access to legal remedies.
- Reasonable judicial proceedings.
- Transparent administrative decisions.
- Effective protection against arbitrary state action.
- Adequate resources for courts and legal institutions.
- Proportionate and evidence-based enforcement.
- Accountability for violations of law by public authorities.

Fundamental rights are not administrative conveniences. They are binding constraints on public power.

## 8. Health and Social Development

A modern society should enable people to live healthy, autonomous and dignified lives.

We support:

- Accessible and high-quality healthcare.
- Prevention and health education.
- Improved coordination between healthcare institutions.
- Responsible use of digital health technologies.
- Adequate working conditions for healthcare professionals.
- Social protection against severe hardship.
- Equal opportunities for participation in society.
- Evidence-based evaluation of healthcare policy.

## 9. Environment and Sustainability

Long-term prosperity requires responsible stewardship of natural resources.

We propose:

- Reducing environmental damage.
- Accelerating the transition to sustainable energy systems.
- Improving energy efficiency.
- Developing sustainable transport and infrastructure.
- Protecting biodiversity and ecosystems.
- Encouraging circular economic models.
- Assessing environmental consequences in public investment decisions.

## 10. Infrastructure and Mobility

Infrastructure is a foundation of economic and social participation.

We seek:

- Reliable public transport.
- Modern railway and road infrastructure.
- High-speed digital connectivity.
- Resilient energy networks.
- Sustainable urban development.
- Accessible public spaces.
- Long-term maintenance and investment planning.

## 11. Europe and International Cooperation

Modern political challenges require cooperation beyond national borders.

We support:

- Democratic cooperation within Europe.
- Respect for international law.
- Peaceful conflict resolution.
- Scientific and educational exchange.
- International trade under fair and transparent conditions.
- Cooperation on environmental and technological challenges.
- Human rights and international humanitarian principles.

## 12. Security and Resilience

Security must protect both individuals and democratic institutions.

We support:

- Effective civil protection.
- Cybersecurity.
- Resilient critical infrastructure.
- Democratic oversight of security institutions.
- Evidence-based crime prevention.
- Proportionate law enforcement.
- International cooperation against serious threats.

## 13. Evaluation of Political Proposals

Every substantial policy proposal should contain:

1. A clear description of the problem.
2. Supporting evidence and sources.
3. The proposed intervention.
4. Legal and constitutional considerations.
5. Expected social and economic effects.
6. Financial implications.
7. Potential risks and unintended consequences.
8. Measurable evaluation criteria.

Where evidence is insufficient, uncertainty must be stated openly.

## 14. Continuous Development

This programme is not immutable.

Every policy area should remain open to new evidence, constructive criticism and democratic revision.

The objective is not ideological consistency at any cost. The objective is to develop better answers to real problems.
