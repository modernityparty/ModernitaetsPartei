## Preamble

The Modernity Party (MP) understands modernity as an ongoing process of human understanding, social development and institutional renewal.

The purpose of politics is to improve people's living conditions, protect their freedom and dignity, and create the foundations for a just, peaceful and sustainable future.

State and societal institutions must be guided by people's actual needs, the state of scientific knowledge, and the possibilities offered by technological and social development. Their legitimacy rests on democratic participation, the rule of law, transparency and accountable responsibility.

The Modernity Party advocates a form of politics that examines problems objectively, bases decisions on verifiable knowledge and critically evaluates their outcomes. It regards neither existing conditions as fundamentally immutable nor innovation as an end in itself.

It is committed to human dignity, freedom and responsibility, democratic self-determination, equality before the law, the separation of powers and fundamental human rights.

Political decision-making within the organisation shall be democratic, transparent, pluralistic and open to constructive criticism. Different views shall be respected as an integral part of democratic development.

The Modernity Party understands itself as an internationally oriented political project. It promotes cooperation across national borders while taking into account the respective constitutional and legal frameworks governing national organisations.

This Constitution establishes the organisational and democratic framework of the Modernity Party. It is further specified by the Founding Principles, the Political Programme and supplementary regulations.

The organisation commits itself to the continuous review and development of its political and institutional foundations.

**Modernity means responsibility: shaping a better future without losing sight of the dignity of people living in the present.**

## § 1 – Name, Registered Seat, Area of Activity and Organisational Status

**(1) Name**

The organisation shall be known as the **Modernity Party**, with the German name **Modernitätspartei**. Its short designation shall be **MP**.

**(2) International Orientation**

The Modernity Party is an internationally oriented political project. Its activities are not restricted to the territory of the Federal Republic of Germany or any other individual state.

**(3) Registered Seat**

The registered seat of a legally independent national organisation shall be determined by its own statutes and in accordance with the applicable law.

A common international administrative seat may be established by a separate democratic resolution.

**(4) National Organisations**

Independent national organisations may be established in individual countries. Such organisations must fulfil the respective constitutional and legal requirements.

Their legal independence, organisational form and responsibilities shall be governed by their own statutes.

**(5) Relationship between the International Project and National Organisations**

The international principles and political objectives shall constitute a common substantive framework.

National organisations shall conduct their political and organisational activities independently within this framework and in accordance with the applicable law.

**(6) Legal Status**

The designation Modernity Party or Modernitätspartei does not, in itself, establish recognition as a political party, legal entity or other organisation possessing legal capacity.

The establishment, registration and recognition of national organisations shall be governed by the respective national laws.

**(7) Transparency**

Organisational status, responsibilities, powers of representation and legal accountability shall be presented publicly and in a comprehensible manner.

## § 2 – Purpose and Political Orientation

**(1) Purpose**

The purpose of the Modernity Party is the democratic development, shaping and renewal of political, social and state structures in the interests of people and future generations.

It seeks to make political decision-making more objective, transparent and effective, and more closely aligned with scientific knowledge and societal needs.

**(2) Human Dignity and Individual Freedom**

The Modernity Party places human dignity at the centre of its political activities.

It advocates individual freedom, personal self-determination, equal rights and opportunities, and the assumption of social responsibility.

**(3) Democracy and the Rule of Law**

The Modernity Party is committed to democratic government, popular sovereignty, the separation of powers, judicial independence and the obligation of state authorities to act in accordance with the law.

It rejects arbitrary political action, abuse of power and restrictions on fundamental democratic rights.

**(4) Science and Knowledge**

Political proposals and decisions should be based on verifiable facts, reasoned arguments and available scientific knowledge.

Scientific findings must be regularly reviewed and must not be appropriated for political purposes irrespective of their quality or evidential basis.

**(5) Progress and Innovation**

The Modernity Party promotes scientific, technological, economic and social progress.

Innovation should serve humanity, be developed responsibly and be assessed with regard to its effects on society, the environment and future generations.

**(6) Social Responsibility and Sustainability**

The Modernity Party advocates social participation, fair living conditions, social security and the responsible use of natural and economic resources.

The interests of present and future generations must be given appropriate consideration in political decisions.

**(7) International Cooperation**

The Modernity Party promotes peaceful international cooperation, respect for international law, the protection of human rights and democratic development across national borders.

**(8) Political Independence and Pluralism**

The Modernity Party is open to different democratic convictions, objective criticism and competing political proposals.

It does not regard political positions as immutable dogmas. What matters is their justification, their compatibility with the organisation's fundamental principles and their effects on people.

**(9) Further Specification**

Political objectives and measures shall be specified in the Political Programme and in supplementary political programmes, resolutions and proposals.

These documents must be consistent with this Constitution and the Founding Principles.

## § 3 – Principles of Internal Party Democracy

**(1) Democratic Decision-Making**

The Modernity Party shall organise its internal affairs in accordance with democratic principles.

Political decision-making shall originate with the members. All bodies and officeholders shall be bound by the applicable principles, statutes and democratically adopted resolutions.

**(2) Equality of Members**

All members shall enjoy the same fundamental membership rights and obligations under the applicable statutes.

Differences in office, experience, financial contribution or organisational responsibility shall not confer additional general voting rights.

**(3) Freedom of Opinion and Protection of Dissent**

Every member shall have the right to express political views freely, submit motions, criticise existing positions and propose alternatives.

Internal criticism shall not, by itself, result in disadvantage merely because it is directed against existing majorities, resolutions or officeholders.

No member shall be deprived of membership rights, opportunities to participate or the right to stand for election solely because of a dissenting political opinion, criticism of the leadership or opposition to a majority resolution.

**(4) Elections and Votes**

Officeholders and representative bodies shall be elected through democratic, transparent and verifiable procedures.

Decisions shall generally be made by vote. Election and voting procedures must ensure fair participation, clear results and appropriate documentation.

Secret ballots shall be conducted wherever required by law.

**(5) Transparency and Accountability**

The basis of decisions, responsibilities, resolutions and significant activities of organisational bodies shall be documented in a manner accessible to members.

Officeholders shall be required to report regularly on their activities.

Statutory data protection, confidentiality and security requirements shall remain unaffected.

**(6) Protection of Minorities**

Democratic majority decisions shall be respected. At the same time, the rights of political minorities and dissenting views shall be protected.

Minorities must be given appropriate opportunities to submit motions, present their positions and request renewed consideration of disputed matters.

**(7) Limitation and Oversight of Power**

Organisational powers and decision-making authority must be clearly assigned and subject to oversight.

The combination of different functions shall be permissible only insofar as it is compatible with the applicable statutes and legal requirements.

Conflicts of interest must be disclosed. Decisions must not be abused for personal enrichment or the unjustified favouring of individuals.

**(8) Removability and Accountability**

Elected officeholders may be removed from office in accordance with the applicable statutes and legal provisions.

Fair procedures, the right to be heard and verifiable grounds for decisions must be ensured.

**(9) Digital Participation**

The Modernity Party promotes digital means of information, discussion, motion submission and political participation.

Digital participation procedures must be appropriately designed with regard to accessibility, data protection, information security, identity verification and protection against manipulation.

They shall complement democratic organisational procedures and shall not replace legally prescribed forms of decision-making.

**(10) Further Development of Democratic Procedures**

The democratic structures and procedures of the Modernity Party shall be regularly reviewed for effectiveness, fairness and accessibility, and further developed where necessary.

## § 4 – Relationship with the Founding Principles and the Political Programme

**(1) Fundamental Documents**

The Modernity Party shall base its political and organisational activities in particular on the following documents:

1. **The Constitution:** It governs the organisation's fundamental identity, organisational order, responsibilities and democratic procedures.
2. **The Founding Principles:** They set out the fundamental political and ethical convictions of the Modernity Party.
3. **The Political Programme:** It describes the political objectives, priorities and intended social developments.
4. **Supplementary Programmes and Resolutions:** They specify individual policy areas and establish particular positions and measures.
5. **Statutes and Supplementary Regulations:** They govern the legal and organisational details required by the respective national organisations.

**(2) Relationship and Scope**

These documents serve different purposes and shall be interpreted in relation to one another.

The Constitution establishes the fundamental organisational and democratic framework. The Founding Principles determine the ethical and political orientation. The Political Programme and supplementary resolutions specify the political objectives derived from them.

The statutes govern the binding organisational structure of the respective legally independent national organisations. They must comply with mandatory legal requirements.

**(3) Compatibility and Precedence**

Within the common international framework of the project, political programmes, individual resolutions and supplementary organisational regulations must be compatible with the fundamental principles and minimum democratic requirements of this Constitution.

For legally independent national organisations, mandatory national legal provisions shall take precedence. Their legally binding statutes shall determine the specific organisation of their structures insofar as this Constitution and the applicable law permit.

Political programmes must not override membership rights or democratic procedural safeguards.

**(4) Amendment and Further Development**

The Political Programme and supplementary political resolutions may be amended, supplemented or replaced through the designated democratic procedures.

Amendments to the Founding Principles and this Constitution require a separate, transparent and democratically legitimate decision-making procedure.

**(5) Public Documentation**

The currently applicable versions of the fundamental documents shall be made publicly accessible.

Amendments must be documented in a verifiable manner. Previous versions should be archived so that the historical development of political and organisational positions remains traceable.

**(6) Open Political Development**

The Modernity Party regards its political documents as foundations that can be reviewed and further developed.

New knowledge, social developments and substantiated criticism may provide grounds for amendments. Further development must take place through orderly democratic procedures.

## § 5 – Membership

**(1) Principle**

Membership in a legally organised national branch of the Modernity Party shall be voluntary.

It shall be open to all persons who recognise the organisation's fundamental principles, respect its democratic order and fulfil the applicable membership requirements.

**(2) Admission**

Admission shall take place upon written or electronic application to the body responsible under the relevant statutes.

Admission requirements and procedures must be transparent, objective and non-discriminatory.

A refusal shall be justified in a comprehensible manner on the basis of the applicable membership rules.

**(3) Membership Rights**

Members shall have, in particular, the right:

1. to participate in internal party decision-making;
2. to submit motions and political proposals;
3. to participate in members' assemblies and designated votes;
4. to vote and stand for election where the relevant requirements are met;
5. to access fundamental documents and resolutions affecting them;
6. to express internal criticism and represent alternative views;
7. to engage politically and organisationally within the organisation;
8. to declare the termination of their membership.

The exercise of these rights shall be governed by this Constitution, the respective statutes and applicable law.

**(4) Membership Obligations**

Members shall, in particular, undertake:

1. to respect human dignity and fundamental democratic principles;
2. to respect the applicable Constitution and lawful democratic resolutions;
3. to respect the rights of other members;
4. to fulfil the organisational and financial obligations associated with their respective membership;
5. to disclose conflicts of interest relevant to their functions;
6. not to deliberately damage the organisation through false statements, manipulation or abusive conduct.

**(5) Equality and Independence**

Membership rights must not be made dependent on wealth, income, professional status or financial support.

Donations and other financial contributions shall not confer additional voting rights or special political decision-making powers.

**(6) Supporters and Contributors**

Participation in public discussions, collaboration on research and programme projects, contributions to digital platforms or support for the Modernity Party shall not, in themselves, constitute formal party membership.

In particular, GitHub contributions, project collaboration, votes on digital platforms or public declarations of support shall not confer statutory membership, voting or signature rights.

The respective forms of participation must be clearly distinguished from one another.

**(7) Termination of Membership**

Membership shall end in particular through:

1. voluntary resignation;
2. the death of the member;
3. expulsion following a proper procedure;
4. other grounds for termination provided for by law or the applicable statutes.

Resignation must be possible at any time in accordance with the applicable statutory and legal provisions.

**(8) Expulsion**

Expulsion shall be permissible only on objectively justified grounds specified in the relevant statutes.

It shall require a fair procedure in which the member concerned is informed of the allegations, given an opportunity to respond and entitled to request a reasoned decision.

An appropriate internal remedy against an expulsion decision must be provided.

**(9) Data Protection**

Personal membership data may be processed only in accordance with applicable data protection provisions and for legitimate organisational purposes.

Membership data must be protected against unauthorised access, publication and misuse.

**(10) Supplementary Membership Regulations**

Details concerning admission, membership, rights, obligations, contributions and termination shall be specified in the statutes of the respective national organisations.

## § 6 – Bodies and Organisational Structure

**(1) Principle**

The Modernity Party shall conduct its activities through democratically legitimised bodies with clearly defined responsibilities.

The specific structure of the bodies of a national organisation shall be determined by its statutes and must comply with applicable legal requirements.

**(2) Fundamental Bodies**

The organisational structure of a national organisation shall include at least the following, insofar as required by the applicable law:

1. the members' assembly or the competent members' body;
2. the executive board;
3. an independent financial audit or oversight body.

Additional bodies may be established through the statutes.

**(3) Members' Assembly**

The members' assembly shall be the fundamental body of internal party decision-making.

Its principal responsibilities shall include:

1. electing and removing the executive board;
2. discussing and adopting resolutions on fundamental political matters;
3. adopting and amending the Political Programme;
4. adopting and amending statutes;
5. receiving activity and financial reports;
6. deciding on fundamental organisational matters;
7. deciding on the dissolution of the organisation, insofar as legally permissible.

**(4) Executive Board**

The executive board shall conduct the organisation's ongoing business and represent it within the scope of its statutory and legal authority.

Its responsibilities shall include, in particular:

1. implementing democratically adopted resolutions;
2. organisational and administrative management;
3. coordinating the various areas of activity;
4. managing financial and material resources;
5. exercising legal representation;
6. regularly reporting to members.

The executive board shall be bound by the applicable statutes, the organisation's fundamental principles and the resolutions of the competent democratic bodies.

**(5) Financial Audit and Oversight**

Financial activities shall be reviewed by an oversight body independent of day-to-day management.

The oversight body shall be appointed democratically in accordance with the respective statutes. Persons whose own management or financial decisions are subject to review must not themselves supervise or decisively influence that review.

The oversight body must have access to the documents necessary for its responsibilities and must be able to report its findings directly to the competent members or bodies.

**(6) Working Groups and Specialist Departments**

Working groups and specialist departments may be established to develop and address political, scientific, technical and organisational matters.

They may include, in particular, the following areas:

- Democracy and State Institutional Modernisation;
- Economy and Innovation;
- Education and Science;
- Environment and Sustainability;
- Health and Social Affairs;
- Justice and the Rule of Law;
- Digitalisation and Technology;
- International Cooperation.

Working groups shall develop analyses, proposals and recommendations. They shall have no independent authority to establish binding political positions for the organisation unless such authority has been expressly delegated to them by a competent democratic body.

**(7) Officers and Special Functions**

Special officers may be appointed for clearly defined responsibilities.

These may include data protection, information security, transparency, equality, member support and international coordination.

Their tasks, responsibilities and reporting obligations must be clearly defined and documented.

**(8) Separation of Powers and Independence**

Decision-making authority, management and oversight shall be organisationally separated in such a way as to limit conflicts of interest and concentrations of power.

In particular, financial oversight must not be subject to the sole authority of those whose financial activities it reviews.

Oversight bodies must be able to perform their duties independently and without improper influence.

**(9) Documentation**

The establishment, composition, responsibilities and decisions of organisational bodies shall be documented in a verifiable manner.

Significant decisions and activity reports should be made accessible to members unless statutory data protection, confidentiality or security considerations prevent this.

**(10) National and International Cooperation**

National organisations may establish joint international working groups, specialist committees and coordination structures.

Such structures shall not automatically possess authority to issue instructions to legally independent national organisations.

Their tasks and powers require an explicit agreement or democratic legitimisation.

## § 7 – Elections, Terms of Office and Democratic Decision-Making Procedures

**(1) Democratic Legitimacy**

All elected officeholders shall derive their authority from democratic elections.

Elections and votes must be conducted freely, equally, transparently and verifiably. The applicable legal requirements must be observed.

**(2) Eligibility to Vote**

Members entitled to vote under the respective statutes and applicable law shall be eligible to vote.

Each eligible member shall generally have one vote. The delegation or multiplication of voting rights shall be permissible only insofar as expressly allowed by applicable legal provisions.

**(3) Election Procedures**

Elections to executive boards and comparable leadership positions shall generally be conducted by secret ballot.

Election procedures must ensure that:

1. all eligible voters are informed of the election in good time;
2. suitable candidacies are possible;
3. votes are cast freely and without pressure or improper influence;
4. votes are properly recorded and counted;
5. the result is established and announced in a verifiable manner.

**(4) Terms of Office**

The terms of office of elected officials shall be specified in the respective statutes.

They must be limited in duration and allow for regular democratic confirmation of the responsibilities entrusted to officeholders.

Immediate re-election shall generally be permissible unless the respective statutes provide for a limit.

**(5) Early Termination of Office**

Elected officeholders may be removed by the competent democratic body in accordance with the applicable statutes and legal provisions.

Any such motion must be dealt with through a transparent procedure. The person concerned must be given an opportunity to respond.

**(6) Extraordinary Elections**

If a member of an elected body leaves office prematurely or the body loses its ability to make decisions, appropriate measures shall be taken in accordance with the respective statutes to restore a democratically legitimised composition.

These measures may include by-elections, new elections or temporary acting arrangements.

Temporary acting appointments must not be used to circumvent a necessary democratic election indefinitely.

**(7) Majorities and Resolutions**

Ordinary resolutions shall generally be adopted by a simple majority of valid votes cast, unless this Constitution, the respective statutes or applicable law requires a different majority.

Amendments to this Constitution shall be governed by § 18. The dissolution of a national organisation shall be governed by § 20.

Abstentions and invalid votes shall be recorded separately when determining the result.

**(8) Right of Motion and Initiative**

Every member shall have the right to submit motions for discussion and decision in accordance with the respective statutes.

The statutes must provide appropriate procedures and deadlines to ensure that motions are considered and not arbitrarily excluded from the agenda.

**(9) Digital and Hybrid Procedures**

Digital and hybrid elections, votes and assemblies may be used insofar as legally permissible.

In particular, identity, voting eligibility, confidentiality, integrity of the vote and verifiability of results must be ensured.

Digital participation must not unduly exclude members who lack adequate technical access.

**(10) Documentation and Challenges**

Election and voting results shall be documented, including the essential procedural details.

Members must have the opportunity to raise substantiated objections to the proper conduct of an election or voting procedure within reasonable time limits.

Objections shall be decided by a competent and, where possible, independent body following a fair procedure.

**(11) Binding Legal Requirements**

The specific design of election and voting procedures shall be governed by the respective statutes and mandatory legal provisions of the country concerned.

This Constitution does not authorise the replacement of legally prescribed election or assembly procedures with informal digital processes.

## § 8 – Transparency, Accountability and Access to Information

**(1) Principle of Transparency**

The Modernity Party commits itself to transparent, comprehensible and responsible organisational management.

Political decisions, organisational responsibilities and the use of shared resources should be understandable to members and the public.

**(2) Publication of Fundamental Documents**

The currently applicable versions of the following documents shall be made publicly accessible:

1. Constitution;
2. Founding Principles;
3. Political Programme and supplementary political programmes;
4. statutes and essential organisational regulations;
5. fundamental resolutions and political statements;
6. codes of conduct and contribution guidelines.

Amendments and previous versions should be documented in a verifiable version history.

**(3) Documentation of Decisions**

Resolutions of the competent bodies shall be recorded in writing.

The documentation should include at least:

1. the body making the decision;
2. the date and subject of the decision;
3. the essential grounds for the decision;
4. the established voting result;
5. where applicable, the responsibilities for implementation.

Personal or confidential information may be protected for legally justified reasons.

**(4) Accountability**

Elected officeholders and organisational bodies shall be required to report regularly on their activities.

Reports should address, in particular, the implementation of adopted resolutions, significant organisational developments, outstanding tasks and existing difficulties.

**(5) Financial Transparency**

Income, expenditure, assets and financial obligations shall be properly recorded and disclosed in accordance with applicable legal requirements.

Members should be given appropriate access to information about the financial situation and use of resources of their organisation.

Legally required accountability reports and audits must be prepared and submitted on time.

**(6) Members' Right to Information**

Members shall be entitled to access the information and documents they need to exercise their membership rights.

Restrictions must be based on objective and legally permissible grounds. They must be justified in a comprehensible manner.

**(7) Public Communication**

Public statements on behalf of the organisation may be made only by persons or bodies authorised to do so.

The organisation should communicate its political positions, decision-making processes and significant activities in a clear and accessible manner.

The personal views of individual members must be distinguished from officially adopted positions.

**(8) Digital Documentation and Open Development**

The Modernity Party promotes the use of open digital tools to document and further develop its political and organisational work.

These may include public repositories, version control systems, discussion platforms, resolution registers and digital archives.

The use of such tools must take data protection, information security, accessibility and the binding nature of formal democratic procedures into account.

**(9) Data Protection and Information Security**

Personal data shall be processed in accordance with the applicable data protection provisions. Only data necessary for a specified and lawful purpose may be collected and used.

The organisation must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect against unauthorised access, loss, alteration and misuse of information.

Access rights, retention periods and responsibilities must be clearly defined and documented.

Membership data and confidential communications must not be published merely because the organisation generally advocates transparency.

**(10) Protection of Confidential Information and Oversight**

Confidential and security-sensitive information shall be protected against unauthorised access only to the extent necessary and legally permissible.

Confidentiality must not be invoked to shield unlawful conduct, abuse of power or objectively unjustified decisions from appropriate scrutiny.

**(11) Review and Improvement**

Transparency, data protection and information security procedures shall be regularly reviewed for completeness, clarity and effectiveness.

Identified deficiencies should be documented and remedied through appropriate measures.

## § 9 – Finance, Asset Management and Economic Independence

**(1) Principle of Financial Responsibility**

The Modernity Party shall manage its financial and material resources economically, transparently and responsibly.

The use of shared resources must serve the democratically adopted objectives and tasks of the organisation.

**(2) Sources of Funding**

Funding may be obtained in particular through:

1. membership contributions;
2. voluntary donations;
3. public funds permitted by law;
4. income from events and publications;
5. other legally permissible sources of income.

All sources of funding must comply with applicable legal requirements.

**(3) Political Independence**

Financial contributions shall not confer special political rights, additional votes or direct influence over democratic decisions.

The organisation must not allow its political positions and resolutions to be determined by individual economic interests or financial dependencies.

**(4) Transparency of Funding**

Income and expenditure shall be documented completely and verifiably.

Donations and other contributions must be recorded, reviewed and, where required, published in accordance with applicable legal provisions.

Impermissible contributions or contributions that cannot legally be accepted must not be accepted or retained.

**(5) Budget Planning**

Each legally independent national organisation shall provide for appropriate budget planning in accordance with its statutes and applicable law.

Significant financial obligations and expenditure must be approved by the respective competent bodies.

**(6) Asset Management**

The organisation's assets shall be managed separately from the private assets of its members and officeholders.

Financial resources may be used exclusively for lawful organisational, political and charitable or party-political purposes, insofar as these are compatible with the organisation's legal status.

**(7) Financial Oversight**

Financial management shall be subject to regular and independent review.

The competent oversight body must have access to the necessary financial records and be able to document its findings.

Identified irregularities shall be reported to the competent bodies and addressed appropriately.

**(8) Conflicts of Interest**

Members and officeholders must disclose personal or economic interests if these could influence their decisions or the use of organisational resources.

Participation in decisions concerning their own direct economic benefits shall be restricted by appropriate rules.

**(9) Remuneration and Reimbursement of Expenses**

Remuneration for activities within the organisation and reimbursement of necessary expenses must be based on a verifiable legal and organisational framework.

The relevant rules shall be documented transparently and subject to appropriate oversight.

**(10) International Financial Relationships**

Financial relationships between international project structures and national organisations must be organised lawfully, transparently and in a verifiable manner.

The independence of national organisations and their respective national funding and accountability obligations shall remain unaffected.

**(11) Legal Requirements**

Financial management shall comply with the applicable legal provisions, particularly those concerning party financing, acceptance of donations, accounting, financial reporting and taxation.

Stricter legal requirements shall take precedence over conflicting internal rules.

## § 10 – Conflicts of Interest, Integrity and Responsibility of Officeholders

**(1) Principle of Personal Integrity**

All members and officeholders shall act honestly, responsibly and in the interests of the democratic objectives of the Modernity Party when exercising their organisational responsibilities.

Personal, economic or institutional interests must not improperly influence the proper performance of assigned duties.

**(2) Disclosure of Conflicts of Interest**

Members and officeholders shall be required to disclose actual or potential conflicts of interest where these may be relevant to an upcoming decision or the exercise of their function.

Disclosure must be made to the respective competent body.

**(3) Handling Conflicts of Interest**

Where a conflict of interest has been identified, appropriate measures shall be taken to ensure the independence and credibility of the decision.

These measures may include, in particular:

1. disclosure to the competent bodies;
2. non-participation in a specific decision;
3. transfer of the relevant task to an impartial person;
4. independent review of the decision;
5. other appropriate organisational safeguards.

**(4) Prohibition of Abuse of Office**

Organisational functions and decision-making powers must not be abused for personal enrichment, intimidation of other members or the pursuit of improper interests.

The following shall be prohibited in particular:

1. misuse of organisational resources;
2. improper influence over elections and votes;
3. suppression of legitimate internal criticism;
4. obtaining unjustified personal or economic benefits;
5. deliberate manipulation of organisational decisions or accountability records.

**(5) Accountability of Officeholders**

Officeholders bear particular responsibility for the lawful, transparent and diligent exercise of their duties.

They shall be required to observe their responsibilities, document decisions in a verifiable manner and report to the competent bodies.

**(6) Protection against Corruption and Improper Influence**

The Modernity Party rejects corruption, bribery, the improper granting of benefits and other forms of abusive influence.

The organisation shall establish appropriate procedures to identify, report and investigate such risks at an early stage.

**(7) Reporting and Protection of Whistleblowers**

Members and employees should be given the opportunity to submit substantiated reports of serious violations confidentially to a competent body.

Persons making such reports shall be protected against unjustified disadvantage, intimidation and retaliation.

Reports must be handled impartially, confidentially and transparently.

**(8) Investigation of Violations**

Reports of possible violations of this Constitution or other binding organisational rules shall be examined through a fair and appropriate procedure.

Persons concerned shall be entitled to information about the allegations against them and an opportunity to respond, insofar as this does not improperly compromise the investigation.

**(9) Consequences**

Proven violations may result in organisational or membership-related consequences in accordance with their severity and the applicable statutes.

Measures must be proportionate and must not be used to suppress legitimate differences of opinion or objective criticism.

**(10) Equality under Organisational Rules**

The above obligations shall apply regardless of office, function, length of membership, personal influence or financial importance to the organisation.

No officeholder shall be exempt from oversight and accountability.

## § 11 – Internal Remedies, Conflict Resolution and Disciplinary Proceedings

**(1) Principle of Fair Procedures**

The Modernity Party shall ensure fair, impartial and comprehensible procedures within the organisation for dealing with conflicts, complaints and possible violations of rules.

No one shall be subjected to arbitrary, discriminatory or disproportionate measures.

**(2) Right to Be Heard**

Every member shall have the right, before a decision adversely affecting them is made, to be informed of the essential allegations and grounds for the decision and to respond to them.

The response must be given appropriate consideration in the decision-making process.

**(3) Impartiality**

Decisions concerning complaints and disciplinary measures must be made by impartial persons or competent bodies.

Persons directly involved in the matter or having a substantial personal interest in its outcome must not participate in the decision.

**(4) Right to Complain**

Members may submit complaints in particular against:

1. decisions and measures taken by officeholders;
2. violations of their membership rights;
3. violations of this Constitution or binding organisational rules;
4. improper restrictions on their participation and involvement;
5. possible cases of abuse of power, discrimination or conflicts of interest.

Complaints must be received and handled through a transparent procedure.

**(5) Independent Review**

Decisions adversely affecting members and complaints concerning the conduct of officeholders should be subject to review by a body that is organisationally and personally impartial.

The reviewing body must not be dependent on the persons whose decisions or conduct are the subject of the complaint.

Where no independent internal body exists or has jurisdiction, an appropriate procedure must be provided for appointing impartial reviewers.

**(6) Conflict Resolution**

Suitable procedures for objective clarification and consensual resolution should initially be offered in cases of internal organisational conflict.

However, mediation or conciliation must not be imposed and must not restrict statutory or legal rights.

**(7) Disciplinary Measures**

Where violations of binding organisational rules have been established, the respective statutes may provide for the following measures in particular:

1. notice or admonition;
2. formal warning;
3. temporary or limited restriction of specific functions;
4. removal from an assigned office;
5. expulsion from the organisation.

The measure selected must be appropriate to the circumstances of the individual case and the severity of the violation.

**(8) Reasons and Documentation**

Decisions adversely affecting members must be justified and documented in writing.

The reasons should identify the established facts, the applicable rules and the essential considerations underlying the decision.

**(9) Appeal**

An appropriate remedy must be available against decisions adversely affecting members.

The review should be conducted by an organisationally and personally impartial body that was not involved in the original decision.

The respective statutes must specify the competent body, time limits and procedure for such remedies.

**(10) Protection against Retaliation and Protection of Dissent**

No one shall be disadvantaged for submitting a complaint in good faith, reporting possible misconduct or exercising democratic membership rights.

In particular, no member shall lose membership rights, opportunities to participate or the right to stand for election solely because of a dissenting political opinion, criticism of the leadership or opposition to a majority resolution.

**(11) Confidentiality and Data Protection**

Personal information arising from complaint and disciplinary proceedings shall be treated confidentially.

Publication shall be permitted only where it is lawful, objectively justified and necessary for the protection of legitimate interests.

**(12) Relationship with State Legal Remedies**

Internal complaint and conciliation procedures shall not replace access to state courts or other legally provided remedies.

The legal rights of members shall remain unaffected.

**(13) Supplementary Procedural Regulations**

Details concerning conflict resolution, complaint procedures and disciplinary measures shall be governed by separate procedural regulations and the respective national statutes.

## § 12 – International Cooperation and National Autonomy

**(1) International Orientation**

The Modernity Party understands itself as an internationally oriented political project whose purpose is to promote democratic cooperation, the exchange of political knowledge and common social development across national borders.

Its fundamental political principles are not restricted to any single state, particular region or national political tradition.

**(2) Common Fundamental Principles**

International cooperation shall be based on the Constitution and the Founding Principles of the Modernity Party.

These documents constitute a common substantive framework. They do not, in themselves, establish any legal authority to issue instructions to national organisations.

**(3) National Autonomy**

Legally independent national organisations shall act independently within their respective constitutional and legal systems.

They shall determine their internal organisational structures, democratic procedures and legal representation through their own statutes.

An international coordination structure shall not automatically possess authority to issue instructions to, represent or make decisions on behalf of national organisations.

**(4) International Coordination**

International coordination bodies, specialist committees and joint working groups may be established to promote cooperation.

Their responsibilities may include, in particular:

1. coordinating joint political projects;
2. exchanging scientific and political knowledge;
3. developing common fundamental positions;
4. supporting the establishment of national organisations;
5. coordinating translations and international publications;
6. developing shared digital infrastructure;
7. promoting democratic participation across national borders.

Their responsibilities must be expressly defined and democratically legitimised.

**(5) Equal Cooperation**

International cooperation should be based on mutual respect, equal participation and transparent decision-making procedures.

The size, financial capacity or political significance of a national organisation shall not, in itself, confer superior political decision-making rights.

**(6) Common Political Development**

National organisations may participate in the development and revision of common political principles and programmes.

Divergent national positions may be documented and justified.

Common positions must be legitimised through a previously established democratic procedure. They must be distinguished from the independent political resolutions of national organisations.

**(7) International Membership and Participation**

International collaboration shall generally be open to all persons wishing to participate in the political and organisational development of the project.

Participation in international working groups or digital platforms shall not automatically constitute formal membership in a national organisation.

Membership, voting and representation rights shall be governed by the respective national statutes and applicable legal provisions.

**(8) Legal and Organisational Cooperation**

Agreements between international and national organisational structures must be documented transparently.

They must neither abolish nor improperly restrict the legal independence of national organisations or their statutory responsibilities.

**(9) International Communication and Authoritative Language**

The Modernity Party promotes multilingual, accessible and transparent political communication.

English shall be the authoritative source and reference language for the common international organisational documents. The English version shall govern the interpretation and further development of the shared international framework.

Translations, particularly the German version, shall serve accessibility, information and participation within the respective language communities. They should remain substantively consistent with the English reference version. In the event of discrepancies between the English version and a translation, the English version shall prevail for the purposes of the shared international framework.

Amendments to the English reference version must be documented through a verifiable version history. Translations should be updated promptly and identified by the corresponding version number.

Mandatory national language, form and publication requirements applicable to legally independent national organisations shall remain unaffected. In particular, this provision does not determine the authoritative language of national statutes, administrative proceedings, judicial proceedings or legally required publications.

The Modernity Party aims to make its fundamental documents available in as many languages as possible.

**(10) Peaceful and Democratic Cooperation**

The international activities of the Modernity Party shall be guided by respect for human rights, international law, democratic self-determination and the peaceful resolution of conflicts.

It rejects political violence, intimidation and the use of force to impose political objectives.

## § 13 – Science, Research, Technology and Open Knowledge Development

**(1) Scientific Orientation**

The Modernity Party commits itself to a policy that appropriately considers scientific knowledge, verifiable facts and methodologically transparent analysis.

Political decisions should be based on an objective examination of available knowledge.

**(2) Freedom of Research and Teaching**

The Modernity Party is committed to the freedom of science, research and teaching.

Scientific findings must not be distorted for reasons of political expediency or improperly influenced by organisational or economic pressure.

**(3) Open Research and Access to Knowledge**

The Modernity Party promotes free and, wherever possible, accessible access to scientific findings, research data and publicly funded research results, insofar as legal, ethical and data protection requirements permit.

Open knowledge development should enable the exchange, verification and further development of knowledge relevant to society.

**(4) Transparency of Scientific Foundations**

Political proposals should disclose their essential scientific and empirical foundations.

Sources, methods, assumptions, uncertainties and identifiable conflicts of interest must be documented in a comprehensible manner.

Different scientific views and substantiated uncertainties must be presented appropriately.

**(5) Technological Progress**

The Modernity Party promotes the responsible development and application of new technologies.

Technological innovation should create social benefits, respect human self-determination and be critically assessed with regard to potential risks and long-term effects.

**(6) Artificial Intelligence and Automated Decision-Making Procedures**

The development and application of artificial intelligence and automated systems must be compatible with human dignity, fundamental rights, transparency and democratic accountability.

In particular, decisions with significant effects on individual rights and living conditions must provide appropriate opportunities for oversight, explanation and review.

**(7) Digital Sovereignty and Information Security**

The Modernity Party advocates secure, interoperable and, wherever possible, open digital infrastructure.

Digital dependencies, security risks and the concentration of technological control should be critically examined and limited through appropriate measures.

**(8) Open Source and Open Standards**

The Modernity Party supports the use and development of free and open-source software and open technical standards, particularly where they promote transparency, verifiability, security and social participation.

Publicly funded digital solutions should, wherever possible, be developed on open, verifiable and maintainable foundations.

**(9) Scientific Independence**

Research and advisory activities within the organisation should be independent, methodologically transparent and free from improper political influence.

The political evaluation of scientific findings remains the responsibility of democratically legitimised bodies. Scientific knowledge must not be mistaken for a substitute for democratic decision-making.

**(10) Assessment of Social Impact**

Significant political and technological initiatives should be examined with regard to their social, economic, legal, ecological and ethical effects.

The results of such assessments should inform political decision-making and be reviewed after implementation.

**(11) Continuous Development of Knowledge**

The Modernity Party regards knowledge as something that can be continuously reviewed and expanded.

New findings, disproven assumptions and substantiated criticism must be capable of prompting a revision of existing positions and decisions.

**(12) Documentation and Accessibility**

Research results, political analyses and methodological foundations should, wherever possible, be permanently documented, version-controlled and made publicly accessible.

Scientific integrity, copyright, data protection and legitimate confidentiality interests must be respected.

## § 14 – Political Programme Development and Evaluation of Political Initiatives

**(1) Principle**

The Modernity Party shall develop its political positions through open, transparent and democratically legitimised procedures.

Political initiatives should be based on a clear description of the problem concerned, objective examination and a comprehensible assessment of possible solutions.

**(2) Openness of Political Development**

Members and external contributors may submit political ideas, analyses and concrete proposals for solutions.

The organisation promotes the participation of different professional, social and scientific perspectives.

The submission of a proposal does not create an entitlement to its acceptance or inclusion in an official programme.

**(3) Review of Political Proposals**

Political proposals should be reviewed in particular according to the following criteria:

1. description and definition of the underlying problem;
2. quality and verifiability of the available evidence;
3. legal and constitutional compatibility;
4. expected social and economic effects;
5. financial requirements and consequential costs;
6. ecological and long-term effects;
7. feasibility and necessary organisational measures;
8. potential risks and unintended side effects;
9. suitable criteria for subsequent evaluation of success.

**(4) Expert Assessment**

Relevant working groups, specialist departments and independent experts may be consulted for the review.

Expert opinions should disclose their sources, methods, assumptions and potential conflicts of interest.

Expert recommendations shall not replace democratic decisions by the competent body.

**(5) Transparent Stages of Development**

Political proposals should be assigned a verifiable development status.

These may include the following stages:

1. idea;
2. draft;
3. under review;
4. revised;
5. submitted for decision;
6. adopted;
7. superseded or rejected.

The respective status must make clear whether a proposal is merely under discussion or already represents a binding political position.

**(6) Democratic Adoption**

The adoption, amendment or withdrawal of official political positions shall be carried out by the democratic bodies responsible under the respective statutes.

Resolutions must be documented and distinguished from non-binding contributions to discussion and expert recommendations.

**(7) Minority Positions and Dissenting Assessments**

Divergent expert and political views may be documented, provided that they are compatible with the organisation's fundamental democratic and legal principles.

An objective minority position must not be suppressed solely because it differs from a majority decision.

**(8) Impact Assessment and Funding**

Significant political initiatives should be assessed, before adoption, with regard to their financial requirements and expected effects.

Both immediate costs and long-term financial obligations and potential consequential effects must be considered.

Costs that cannot be conclusively determined, or assumptions that remain uncertain, must be explicitly identified as such.

**(9) Review of Adopted Initiatives**

The implementation of political resolutions should be evaluated using appropriate criteria.

The evaluation should establish whether the intended objectives have been achieved, what effects have actually occurred and whether adjustments are necessary.

**(10) Publication and Version Control**

Political proposals, revision histories, expert opinions and resolutions should, wherever possible, be publicly documented and version-controlled.

Personal data, confidential information and legally protected content shall be excluded.

**(11) Continuous Improvement**

The Modernity Party regards political programme development as an ongoing process of learning and improvement.

Adopted positions may be reconsidered on the basis of new knowledge, changing social conditions and demonstrated shortcomings.

## § 15 – Communication, Public Relations and Political Representation

**(1) Principle of Public Communication**

The Modernity Party commits itself to open, objective and transparent communication with the public, members, the media and social institutions.

**(2) Political Representation**

The organisation shall be represented externally by persons and bodies that have been democratically legitimised or expressly authorised for this purpose.

Powers of representation must be clearly defined and transparent.

**(3) Authority of Public Statements**

Official political statements must be consistent with the organisation's democratically adopted positions.

The personal opinions of members and officeholders must be distinguished from official statements.

**(4) Freedom of the Press and Media**

The Modernity Party is committed to freedom of the press, freedom of expression and the protection of independent journalism.

Critical reporting and public scrutiny of political activities shall be recognised as essential components of democratic societies.

**(5) Digital Public Sphere**

The organisation promotes accessible and transparent digital communication.

Official websites, publications and digital platforms should provide comprehensible access to current information, political documents and democratic resolutions.

**(6) Responsibility and Corrections**

Official statements that are demonstrably false or misleading shall be corrected appropriately.

The organisation commits itself to objective communication and rejects deliberate disinformation, manipulation and personal intimidation.

## § 16 – Equality, Participation and Accessibility

**(1) Equality and Human Dignity**

The Modernity Party respects the equal dignity and fundamental rights of all people.

It rejects discrimination on the basis of personal characteristics, origin, social status or other protected attributes.

**(2) Equal Participation**

All members should have appropriate opportunities to participate in political and organisational activities, regardless of their personal, social or economic circumstances.

**(3) Accessibility**

The Modernity Party advocates making its events, communication channels, digital platforms and publications accessible to the greatest extent reasonably possible.

Different physical, sensory, linguistic and technical requirements should be taken into account.

**(4) Accessibility of Democratic Procedures**

Elections, votes, members' assemblies and other participation procedures should be designed to enable members with different physical, sensory, linguistic and technical requirements to participate on equal terms.

Appropriate accommodations shall be provided within the scope of applicable legal and organisational possibilities.

**(5) Social Participation**

The organisation promotes political and social participation and advocates the removal of unjustified barriers to access.

**(6) Fair Organisational Culture**

Leadership and decision-making structures should promote equal opportunities, diversity and fair participation.

**(7) Protection against Disadvantage**

Discrimination, harassment and unjustified disadvantage within the organisation are incompatible with the fundamental principles of the Modernity Party.

Appropriate complaint and protection procedures must be provided.

## § 17 – Environmental Responsibility and Intergenerational Equity

**(1) Responsibility towards Future Generations**

The Modernity Party recognises the responsibility of present-day societies towards future generations.

Political decisions should give appropriate consideration to their long-term social, ecological and economic effects.

**(2) Protection of Natural Foundations of Life**

The organisation advocates the protection of the natural foundations of life, biodiversity and functioning ecosystems.

**(3) Sustainable Development**

Economic and technological development should be designed to remain compatible with ecological responsibility, resource conservation and long-term social stability.

**(4) Scientific Basis**

Environmental and climate policy should be based on verifiable scientific findings, transparent impact assessments and an objective evaluation of different courses of action.

**(5) Long-Term Perspective**

Political initiatives should take into account long-term consequences, potential irreversible damage and the interests of future generations.

**(6) Implementation and Review**

The Modernity Party commits itself to regularly reviewing its own political proposals and organisational activities with regard to their ecological effects.

## § 18 – Amendment of the Constitution

**(1) Principle of Amendability**

This Constitution may be amended, supplemented or replaced through democratic decision-making.

It is not an immutable document but the fundamental organisational framework of an ongoing process of democratic development.

**(2) Right to Propose Amendments**

Amendment proposals may be submitted by members and by the bodies authorised under the respective statutes.

Proposals must include the proposed wording and a comprehensible justification.

**(3) Transparency and Consultation**

Proposed amendments must be made available to eligible voting members in good time.

Appropriate opportunities for discussion, comments and the submission of further amendments must be ensured.

**(4) Required Majority**

Amendments to this Constitution shall generally require a majority of at least two thirds of the valid votes cast by eligible members or their duly elected representatives.

Stricter legal or statutory requirements shall remain unaffected.

**(5) Protection of the Democratic Order**

Amendments must not be used to abolish human dignity, fundamental human rights, democratic decision-making or minimum requirements of the rule of law.

**(6) Documentation**

Every adopted amendment shall be documented with its date, the body adopting it, the voting result and the wording approved.

Previous versions shall remain accessible in the version archive.

**(7) National Legal Requirements**

The respective statutes of legally independent national organisations and mandatory legal provisions shall apply in addition.

Amendment of this common Constitution shall not replace any resolutions required from national organisations under their statutes or applicable law.

## § 19 – Transitional and Founding Provisions

**(1) Founding Phase**

The Modernity Party may initially conduct its international political and organisational development as an open project.

Publication of this Constitution alone does not establish a legally independent political party or other legal entity.

**(2) Provisional Status**

Until a legally independent national organisation has been formally established, the published organisational documents shall serve as foundations and proposals for its subsequent democratic organisation.

They shall not establish membership rights, powers of representation or statutory party bodies unless these have been validly established under the applicable law.

**(3) Establishment of National Organisations**

National organisations shall be established through the respective democratic and legal procedures.

In particular, the applicable requirements concerning founding assemblies, statutes, membership, elections and legal registration or notification must be observed.

**(4) First Democratic Resolution**

The first formal members' assembly of a national organisation shall decide on the adoption of its statutes and the necessary organisational foundations.

It shall elect the designated bodies and resolve on the further steps required for the lawful commencement of activities.

**(5) Review of the Draft**

Before formal adoption, this Constitution must be reviewed for compatibility with the applicable national law.

Any necessary amendments must be documented transparently.

**(6) Transition to Regular Organisation**

Upon formal establishment and democratic adoption of the relevant organisational documents, the corresponding provisions shall enter into force.

Any transitional arrangements that differ from these provisions must be expressly adopted and limited in time or scope.

## § 20 – Dissolution and Regulation of Assets

**(1) Principle**

The dissolution of a legally independent national organisation may be decided only by the democratic body responsible for this purpose and in accordance with the applicable statutes and law.

**(2) Democratic Decision**

A motion for dissolution must be announced in good time, justified and made accessible to eligible voting members.

The decision shall require a majority of at least two thirds of the valid votes cast, unless applicable law or the statutes impose stricter requirements.

**(3) Winding-Up**

In the event of dissolution, ongoing business shall be wound up properly.

Liabilities, legal obligations, employment relationships and other legitimate claims shall be dealt with in accordance with applicable legal provisions.

**(4) Management of Assets**

Assets remaining after all liabilities have been settled shall be dealt with in accordance with the respective statutes and applicable law.

The use of assets must not result in the unlawful personal enrichment of members or officeholders.

**(5) Documentation and Accountability**

The winding-up process shall be documented in a verifiable manner.

The competent bodies and members shall be informed of the process and outcome to the extent legally permissible.

**(6) International Cooperation**

The dissolution of a national organisation shall not automatically result in the dissolution of the international Modernity Party project or other legally independent national organisations.

Existing agreements and shared obligations shall be dealt with separately.

**(7) Dissolution of the International Project**

The termination of the international project shall be decided through a transparent and democratically legitimised procedure.

Existing third-party rights, contractual obligations, intellectual property rights and legal requirements must be taken into account.

## Final Provision

This Constitution is an open international constitutional draft of the Modernity Party.

It serves as a basis for the shared development of a democratic, transparent and responsible political organisation.

Its publication does not constitute the formal establishment of a political party or its legal adoption as the statutes of a national organisation.

Legal binding effect shall arise only through the respective required democratic decision-making procedures and compliance with applicable law.
